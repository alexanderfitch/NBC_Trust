For many owners, a single client review costs far more time outside the room than inside it. Pulling reports, building the packet, reading old notes, writing the follow-up. None of that is the part your clients value most. They value you, paying attention, remembering what matters to them.

The goal is to keep that, and move everything else off your desk.

## Standardize the packet

Agree on one packet format for every review. The same sections, in the same order, every time. Clients find it easier to follow, and a team member can assemble it without asking you what to include.

Keep anything personal, such as the note you want to raise about a grandchild's education or a recent loss, in a short "talking points" box at the top. That's the only part you write.

## Move preparation to the day before

Ask your team to have the packet and a one-page brief ready the afternoon before. The brief needs only four things:

1. What we discussed last time, and what we promised.
2. What has changed since: life events, service requests, account changes.
3. Anything the client has asked about recently.
4. Anything the team noticed that you should know.

You read it in ten minutes, add your talking points, and walk in prepared.

## Keep a standard agenda

A familiar shape helps clients relax and helps you stay on time. A simple one works well: what's changed in your life, how things stand, what we recommend, what happens next. Leave room for the conversation to wander. That is often where the most useful things are said.

## Close the loop the same day

Decide in the meeting who will do what, and say it out loud. Your team sends the follow-up the same day using a short template, and logs the notes in your client system, not your notebook.

That last step matters more than it looks. Notes in a shared system are what let someone else pick up a relationship when you are on vacation, and one day, permanently.

## What you get back

Most owners who adopt a rhythm like this spend a fraction of the time they used to on each review, and clients usually notice only that things feel more organized. Multiply that across a review season and it adds up to weeks.
