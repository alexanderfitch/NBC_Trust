There is a common assumption that succession planning is something you do at the end: a project for the last few years, mostly about paperwork and price.

The owners who end up with the most choices tend to see it differently. For them, a practice that can run without its founder isn't an exit strategy. It's simply a better-run practice, and they enjoy the benefits for years before they ever think about stepping back.

## Two benefits, one piece of work

Think about what makes a practice easy for someone else to take on:

- Client history and preferences recorded in a shared system
- Processes written down, so the team knows what to do without asking
- Clients who know and trust more than one person at the firm
- Technology that a newcomer could learn in a day
- An owner whose calendar isn't the bottleneck for every decision

Now read the same list as an owner who has no plans to leave. Each item also means fewer interruptions, easier vacations, a calmer team and more time for the work you actually enjoy.

It's the same list. You just get the first benefit long before the second.

## Why starting early matters

The slowest parts of a good handover aren't legal or financial. They're human: introducing clients to new people and letting trust form, training someone to make the calls you make instinctively, and deciding what you really want.

None of that can be rushed in the last year. All of it can be started this year, and it pays you back immediately.

## A gentle place to start

Pick one item from the list above and make it a little more true this quarter. Write one process down. Introduce one long-standing client to a colleague. Move one notebook into your client system.

If you'd like a clearer picture first, the [readiness check](/tools/readiness/) takes about four minutes and nothing you tick is saved.
