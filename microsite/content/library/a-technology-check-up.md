Most small firms don't have a technology problem. They have a joining-up problem: four or five systems that each work fine, and a person who spends hours moving information between them.

This check-up is meant to be done in an afternoon, with whoever on your team uses the systems most.

## 1. Draw the map

On a single sheet of paper, list every system you use: client management, custodian platforms, financial planning, document storage, email, calendar, e-signature, billing. Draw a line between any two that share information, and mark each line *automatic* or *by hand*.

Every line marked "by hand" is time someone spends retyping. That's where to look first.

## 2. Ask four questions of each system

- **Does everyone who needs it actually use it?** A client system only half the team updates is worse than none.
- **Is it the single place for that information?** If client notes live in the system *and* in your notebook, the notebook wins, and nobody else can read it.
- **Could someone new learn it in a day?** If not, it depends on one person.
- **Who could recover it if they left?** Logins, admin rights and renewal dates belong in the manual.

## 3. Fix the handoffs, not the tools

Before buying anything, see whether the tools you already pay for can connect. Many client systems integrate directly with major custodians, calendars and e-signature tools, and those features are often switched off simply because nobody set them up.

## 4. Be thoughtful about new tools

There are genuinely useful new tools for small firms, including services that draft meeting notes or summarize documents. They can save real time. But advisors have recordkeeping, privacy and supervision obligations, so before adopting anything that touches client information, check how it stores data, whether it fits your recordkeeping requirements, and what your compliance process requires. When in doubt, ask your compliance resource first.

## 5. Write down what you decided

Add a single page to your operating manual: what each system is for, who owns it, how to get help, and when it renews. It is a small thing, and it is exactly what anyone stepping into your firm will need on day one.
