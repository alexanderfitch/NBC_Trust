# He built it over decades. Then he had to decide what happened to it.

Dan spent a career becoming the person his clients called first. Then he started thinking about what came after — and realised he had never made a plan for the one thing he could not delegate.

## Before

He had what most good independent advisers have: a practice built almost entirely on trust, a client list full of families he had known through the hardest years of their lives, and no obvious answer to what happened next.

The options he knew about all felt wrong. Selling to an aggregator meant handing his clients to people he had never met. Doing nothing meant leaving the decision to whoever was around when it got made for him.

> The thing that kept me up was not the money. It was the idea of a client calling and getting somebody who did not know their story.

## The decision

What changed was realising the handover could be designed rather than transacted — that he could pick the person, set the pace, and stay long enough to make the introduction himself.

[video:dans-decision]

## The handover

It was not a closing date. It was a period of overlap, deliberately slow, where clients met the person taking over while Dan was still in the room.

The families who had worked with him for decades did not get a letter announcing a change. They got an introduction, from him, to someone he had chosen.

[video:the-handover]

## Afterwards

The clients stayed. Not all of them, and not automatically — but the relationships survived, because they were handed over rather than transferred.

## What he would tell someone else

Start the conversation earlier than feels necessary. Not because anything is urgent, but because having the plan is what makes the timing yours.
